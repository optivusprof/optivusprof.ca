When each model is the right choice

✓ Choose a fractional CMO when:

  • Revenue is $15M–$60M and marketing has outgrown founder-led execution
  • You can't justify $250K+ full-time, or the search would take too long
  • Pipeline is inconsistent and you need strategic leadership quickly
  • A GTM rebuild is needed after a pivot, acquisition, or rebrand
  • You have some marketing execution capacity but lack senior strategic direction
  • You want accountability tied to revenue outcomes, not billable hours

→ Choose a full-time CMO when:

  • Revenue exceeds $60M and the marketing org has 6+ people
  • Marketing is a primary growth driver requiring daily full-time strategic focus
  • The company is preparing for IPO or major institutional investment
  • You need a CMO on the cap table as a long-term strategic partner
  • You have 12+ months to run a rigorous executive search

✓ Choose a fractional CMO as interim when:

  • Your CMO just departed and you need immediate leadership coverage
  • You're in a full-time CMO search and can't leave the function leaderless for 6–12 months
  • Post-merger or post-acquisition GTM integration is needed immediately

→ Choose a consultant when:

  • You need a specific deliverable: a brand audit, competitive analysis, or training program
  • You have strong internal marketing leadership but need specialist input
  • A defined project with a defined end date and output is the requirement
  • Budget is very constrained and advisory is sufficient for now

The accountability gap - why it matters more than cost

The most important distinction between a fractional CMO and a marketing consultant is not the price - it's the accountability structure. A consultant is paid for their time and their deliverables. A fractional CMO is paid to drive outcomes.

When you engage Optivus, Dean Reid owns a pipeline target. He attends your leadership team meeting. He manages your marketing people. He presents marketing performance to your board. If the strategy isn't working, he fixes it - because he's accountable to the result, not the deliverable.

This changes the dynamic entirely. A consultant optimizes for a good-looking presentation. A fractional CMO optimizes for pipeline, because that's what his engagement is measured on.

The question to ask in any CMO engagement - fractional or otherwise: "Will you own a pipeline number?" If the answer is yes, you have a real marketing leader. If the answer is hedged with "I can influence pipeline but can't control sales" - you have an advisor. Both have value, but only one is a CMO.
