The Content Blueprint for High-Impact Demand Generation

What to publish at every stage of the buyer’s journey, from first awareness to loyal advocate, so your content builds qualified pipeline instead of just leads.

Dean Reid  ·  Fractional CMO, Optivus  ·  Published July 2025  ·  Last updated October 2026  ·  8 min read
← Back to Resources
Share LinkedIn X Email

Quick answer

What content should B2B companies create at each stage of the funnel?

At the top of the funnel (TOFU), publish educational blog posts, short videos, infographics, and SEO content. In the middle (MOFU), use case studies, webinars, comparison guides, and interactive tools. At the bottom (BOFU), offer demos, free trials, testimonials, and transparent pricing. After purchase, use onboarding, customer webinars, communities, and referral programs to drive retention and advocacy.

TOFU, MOFU, BOFU: The three stages of the marketing funnel: top of funnel (awareness), middle of funnel (consideration), and bottom of funnel (decision). Each stage needs different content, calls to action, and metrics.

Lifecycle content marketing: A content strategy that serves buyers across the full customer lifecycle, from first awareness through purchase to retention and advocacy, rather than focusing only on acquisition.

Key idea 01

Demand generation works when content, channels and touchpoints are aligned across the whole customer lifecycle, not just the top of the funnel.

Key idea 02

Every stage needs a different job done: spark interest, nurture consideration, support the decision, deepen engagement. One-size content does none of them well.

Key idea 03

The goal is qualified pipeline, not just leads. Measure each stage by whether it moves the right people to the next one.

The blueprint in three lines. The stage-by-stage guide follows.

A successful demand generation strategy aligns content, channels, and touchpoints across the entire customer lifecycle to create a seamless, value-driven experience that drives both acquisition and retention.

Lifecycle content marketing guides prospects from first awareness and brand discovery at the top of the funnel (TOFU), through consideration and evaluation in the middle (MOFU), to the purchase decision at the bottom (BOFU). Done well, it also nurtures existing customers, encouraging repeat business and turning them into loyal advocates.

What is a lifecycle content blueprint?

A lifecycle content blueprint maps what to publish at each stage of the B2B buyer’s journey: awareness (TOFU), consideration (MOFU), decision (BOFU), and post-purchase retention and advocacy, with a goal, content types, calls to action, and metrics for each.

The funnel at a glance

Funnel stageGoalBuyer mindsetExample content
TOFUAwareness“I have a problem.”Blogs, short videos, and ads
MOFUConsideration“I’m exploring solutions.”Webinars, guides, comparison pages
BOFUDecision“Which is the best option for me?”Case studies, free trials, demo offers
Post-purchaseRetention and advocacy“Was this the right choice?”Onboarding content, how-tos, loyalty programs, referral incentives

By delivering the right message at the right time, you spark interest, nurture consideration, support buying decisions, and deepen post-purchase engagement. This guide walks through building an integrated campaign that generates qualified pipeline, not just leads.

What content works at the top of the funnel (TOFU)?

Top-of-funnel content attracts new audiences, sparks interest, and introduces them to your brand. Its job is to educate and engage people who don’t know you yet.

01

Who you’re talking to

  • Unaware of your brand, or only vaguely familiar with it
  • Focused on identifying problems, exploring solutions, or learning something new
  • In the early stage of recognizing a need or problem

Content types that work here

Content typePurposeExample CTA
Blog postsEducate, inform, and solve common problems“Read more”
InfographicsExplain concepts or statistics visually“Learn why”
Explainer videosSimplify complex topics in video form“Watch now”
Social postsReach audiences where they spend time“See more”
SEO contentDrive organic discovery through search“Download”

Build the plan

Brainstorm topics. Identify your ideal prospect’s common questions and pain points, research keywords tied to early-stage queries, and mine Google Trends, AnswerThePublic, and industry forums for ideas.

Outline the content. Use headlines that earn attention, such as “How to…”, “A beginner’s guide to…”, or “Why… matters”, and organize information in clear, scannable sections.

Choose the right CTA. “Read more” encourages further exploration. “Download” offers a checklist or eBook. “Learn why” invites a deeper look at the topic.

Create and distribute

  • Blog posts: roughly 750-1,200 words, focused on solving a problem or teaching something.
  • Infographics: key stats or concepts summarized at a glance.
  • Explainer videos: under 2 minutes for the highest engagement.
  • Social posts: tailored to each platform, whether LinkedIn, X, Facebook, or Instagram.
  • SEO content: built around high-volume, low-difficulty keywords.

Measure success

  • Impressions, clicks, and engagement on each post, video, and asset
  • Keyword rankings and organic traffic trends
  • Which CTAs drive deeper engagement and click-through to the next stage

Example campaign

Content exampleSuggested CTA
“The 7 Signs You Need Better Project Management” (blog post)“Read more”
“How Supply Chains Work” (2-minute explainer video)“Learn why”
“Industry Stats at a Glance” (infographic)“Download”

Best practices

  • Use simple language and avoid jargon.
  • Add images and charts to aid comprehension.
  • Link to deeper content to guide readers down the funnel.
  • Educate, don’t sell.
  • Answer genuine questions and pain points.
  • Give value up front to build trust and credibility.

What content works in the middle of the funnel (MOFU)?

Middle-of-funnel content nurtures leads who know your brand and are actively researching solutions. The goal is to educate, build trust, and position you as a strong contender.

02

Who you’re talking to

  • Problem-aware and weighing several solutions
  • Looking for in-depth information, comparisons, and practical insight
  • Searching for evidence that you address their specific needs

Content types that work here

Content typePurposeExample CTA
Case studiesShow real-world impact and results“See how it works”
WebinarsProvide live, interactive education“Register now”
Comparison guidesPosition you against competitors and alternatives“Compare options”
Product demosIllustrate key features and benefits“Watch demo”
Email newslettersKeep leads engaged and deepen relationships“Get updates”
White papers and guidesOffer detailed research and analysis“Download guide”
Interactive toolsPersonalized assessments or calculators“Try it now”

Build the plan

Brainstorm topics. Pinpoint common evaluation criteria and objections, list feature comparisons, integration options, and ROI justifications, and mine FAQs from your support and sales teams.

Outline and structure. Organize content around the questions buyers are actually asking, with sections like “Why choose us?”, a customer story, or “How we compare.”

Choose the right CTA. “See how it works” invites a walkthrough of real usage. “Compare options” encourages a side-by-side assessment. “Download guide” offers an in-depth resource.

Create and distribute

  • Case studies: a relatable story of transformation and impact.
  • Webinars: tackle industry pain points and host live Q&A.
  • Comparison guides: fair, data-driven competitor comparison tables.
  • Demo videos: under 5 minutes, focused on real use cases.
  • Email series: actionable nurture sequences personalized to interest.

Measure success

  • Downloads, sign-ups, webinar attendance, and email engagement
  • Lead qualification and the rate leads move to the next stage
  • Which CTAs trigger further engagement or conversion

Example campaign

Content exampleSuggested CTA
“How Acme Inc. Reduced Costs 34%” (case study)“See how it works”
“Product X vs. Competitor Y” (comparison guide)“Compare options”
“ROI Calculator” (interactive tool)“Try it now”

Best practices

  • Educate, don’t sell.
  • Use clear, empathetic language that addresses buyer hesitation.
  • Weave in testimonials and data wherever you can.
  • Link to bottom-funnel resources for leads who are ready to convert.

What content works at the bottom of the funnel (BOFU)?

Bottom-of-funnel content serves leads who are ready to buy and need final proof or reassurance. It removes barriers and gives strong reasons to choose you.

03

Who you’re talking to

  • Solution-aware and ready to make a decision
  • Need assurance, proof of value, transparent pricing, and lower risk
  • Want a frictionless path to purchase or a direct sales contact

Content types that work here

Content typePurposeExample CTA
Product demos (live or recorded)Validate fit and functionality“Book a demo”
Free trials or samplesRemove risk with hands-on experience“Start free trial”
Customer testimonialsProvide social proof“Read reviews”
Pricing pages and proposalsGive transparent detail for the decision“See pricing”
Consultations and sales callsOffer personalized guidance“Schedule a call”
Implementation guidesAddress onboarding concerns“Download guide”
Comparison chartsOvercome last-minute objections“See comparison”

Build the plan

Address the final decision points. Map common sales objections, such as ROI proof, technical detail, and security, to specific content. Gather and display authentic testimonials and case wins. Create checklists or onboarding roadmaps that reduce post-purchase anxiety.

Structure for fast action. Make CTAs direct, like “Buy now” or “Request a quote.” Give one-click access to demos, trials, and sales. Remove distractions so the content focuses entirely on readiness to close.

Create and distribute

  • Demos and trials: quick, self-guided, or scheduled at the buyer’s convenience.
  • Testimonial videos: highlight results and satisfaction.
  • Pricing and proposals: clear, jargon-free, with value breakdowns.
  • Consultations: easy online booking and personalized agendas.

Measure success

  • Conversion rates on demo and trial sign-ups, pricing pages, and proposal acceptance
  • Touchpoint interactions and time to close
  • Which testimonials and assets contribute most to closed deals

Example campaign

Content exampleSuggested CTA
“Live Product Tour” (webinar or demo)“Book a demo”
“Customer Story: 300% ROI” (testimonial)“Read reviews”
“Transparent Pricing Comparison” (page)“See pricing”

Best practices

  • Lead with value and relevance in every asset.
  • Provide plenty of credible social proof: reviews, logos, and statistics.
  • Anticipate objections and address them proactively, for example “Why choose us over X?”
  • Use direct CTAs matched to the buyer’s readiness.
  • Use clarity and urgency in your messaging.
  • Use comparison tables and ROI graphics to speed decisions.
  • Keep optimizing based on engagement and conversion data.

The TOFU, MOFU, BOFU framework aligns marketing with the buyer’s journey. It improves lead quality, lifts conversion, sharpens sales alignment and attribution, and makes demand generation revenue-driven.

What content drives post-purchase retention and advocacy?

Once a customer converts, the journey doesn’t end. It evolves. This stage secures satisfaction, drives renewals and upsells, and turns customers into advocates.

04

Who you’re talking to

  • Have invested in your solution and want to see the value
  • Expect support, clear onboarding, and ongoing engagement
  • Can become promoters if delighted, or detractors if neglected

Content types that work here

Content typePurposeExample CTA
Onboarding emails and videosEnsure successful adoption“Watch the onboarding series”
Help center and knowledge baseEnable self-service and reduce friction“Visit the help center”
Customer webinarsDeepen usage and loyalty“Join advanced training”
Success check-insBuild relationships and close gaps“Schedule a success review”
Exclusive communitiesFoster peer learning and connection“Join our community”
Referral programsTurn satisfaction into advocacy“Refer and earn rewards”
Case study invitationsEncourage participation and recognition“Share your story”
Loyalty and rewards programsReward continued engagement“Unlock loyalty benefits”

Build the plan

Deliver the value. Build tailored onboarding journeys with milestones, combine automation with a human touch at key steps, and offer real-time support through chat, Slack channels, or live agents.

Engage and educate continuously. Send 30, 60, and 90-day milestone emails with usage tips, host quarterly success webinars and advanced feature spotlights, and give engaged customers a preview of the roadmap.

Cultivate advocacy. Identify promoters through NPS surveys or support satisfaction scores, invite them to a customer advisory board or beta group, and showcase their success in content, webinars, and speaking opportunities.

Create and distribute

  • Onboarding toolkit: delivered instantly after sign-up.
  • In-app tooltips and walkthroughs: drive feature adoption in context.
  • Video testimonials: co-created success stories with happy clients.
  • Ambassador programs: packaged referral benefits and case study recognition.

Measure success

  • Churn and retention: are customers sticking around?
  • Net Promoter Score: do they recommend you?
  • Customer lifetime value: is value growing over time?
  • Referrals: are customers bringing in others?
  • Expansion revenue: are they buying more?

Example campaign

Content exampleSuggested CTA
“90-Day Success Webinar”“Join advanced training”
“Customer Win: From User to Advocate”“Share your story”
“Exclusive Referral Program Invite”“Refer and earn”

Best practices

  • Overdeliver early to head off buyer’s remorse.
  • Celebrate milestones, such as a customer’s first 100 logins or first 1,000 transactions.
  • Keep educating so users go deeper into the product.
  • Ask for feedback to show commitment and find improvements.
  • Equip champions with branded assets and social sharing kits.

The bottom line

A content-first demand generation strategy delivers consistent, valuable experiences at every stage of the customer journey. Align TOFU, MOFU, BOFU, and post-purchase content with buyer intent and you attract the right audiences, build trust through education, accelerate conversions with confidence-building assets, and turn customers into champions who fuel word-of-mouth growth.

Frequently asked questions

What are TOFU, MOFU and BOFU in B2B marketing?

They’re the three stages of the marketing funnel. Top of funnel (TOFU) builds awareness with people who are just recognizing a problem. Middle of funnel (MOFU) nurtures prospects who are comparing solutions. Bottom of funnel (BOFU) helps ready-to-buy prospects make a confident decision. A complete content strategy adds a fourth, post-purchase stage for retention and advocacy.

What content works best at each funnel stage?

TOFU: blog posts, infographics, short explainer videos and SEO content. MOFU: case studies, webinars, comparison guides, demos and interactive tools. BOFU: live demos, free trials, testimonials, transparent pricing and consultations. Post-purchase: onboarding series, help centers, customer webinars, communities and referral programs.

How do you measure content performance at each stage?

Match the metric to the job. TOFU: impressions, engagement, rankings and click-through to the next stage. MOFU: downloads, webinar attendance, email engagement and lead progression. BOFU: demo and trial conversion, proposal acceptance and time to close. Post-purchase: retention, NPS, lifetime value, referrals and expansion revenue.

Why include post-purchase content in a demand generation strategy?

Because retention and advocacy feed the top of the funnel. Customers who see value early renew, expand and refer others, and their stories become your most credible MOFU and BOFU content.

What is lifecycle content marketing?

Lifecycle content marketing serves buyers across the whole customer lifecycle, from first awareness through purchase to retention and advocacy. It treats post-purchase content as part of demand generation, because satisfied customers renew, expand, and refer others.

Which calls to action work best at each funnel stage?

Use soft CTAs at the top, such as Read more, Learn why, or Download. Use evaluation CTAs in the middle, such as See how it works, Compare options, or Register now. Use direct CTAs at the bottom, such as Book a demo, Start free trial, or See pricing.
